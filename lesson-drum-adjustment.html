Drum Brake Adjustment
Progress · 0%
Slide 1 of 3

Why Drums Need Adjustment

Unlike disc brakes (where the piston moves out as the pads wear), drum brakes need to compensate for shoe wear by moving the shoes outward.

If the shoes are too far from the drum: the brake pedal has to travel further before the shoes contact the drum. The result is a low pedal.
If the shoes are too close to the drum: the brakes drag, generating heat and wearing the shoes prematurely.

The right adjustment puts the shoes just shy of contact with the drum at rest.

Slide 2 of 3

The Self-Adjuster

Modern drum brakes have a self-adjuster mechanism that automatically tightens the shoe spread as they wear. The mechanism is usually:

  • A star wheel adjuster located between the bottoms of the shoes
  • An actuator lever that rotates the star wheel a small amount each time the brakes are applied while reversing
  • (Or on some vehicles, every time the parking brake is applied)
If the self-adjuster mechanism is rusted, broken, or installed wrong, the shoes won't adjust as they wear. The driver will notice a progressively lower pedal until manual adjustment is performed.
Slide 3 of 3

Pre-Adjustment After Service

After installing new shoes (or any drum service that requires backing the adjuster off), you MUST pre-adjust the brakes before driving the vehicle:

  • Turn the star wheel manually until the shoes lightly contact the drum.
  • Back off the star wheel a specified number of clicks (typically 6-10, check service manual).
  • Confirm the drum spins freely with light drag.
  • Pump the pedal several times after installation to allow the self-adjuster to complete the adjustment.
Driving a vehicle with un-adjusted drums means relying on the front disc brakes alone. The pedal will be very low and stopping distance will be much longer than normal.
