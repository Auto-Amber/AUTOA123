Brake Pedal Height, Travel & Free Play

What You're Measuring

Three different brake pedal measurements, three different stories:

  • Pedal height: distance from the floor (or a reference point) to the pedal pad at rest.
  • Free play: distance the pedal moves freely before resistance is felt. This is slack in the linkage.
  • Pedal travel: total distance the pedal moves under braking force.
Always measure with the engine OFF and the brake pedal pumped down to bleed off vacuum reserve before checking height.
Slide 2 of 3

What Abnormal Readings Mean

Common pedal feel problems and what they suggest:

  • Low pedal: air in the system, fluid leak, worn pads/shoes, or out-of-adjustment drums.
  • Hard pedal: failed power booster, vacuum supply problem, restricted brake line.
  • Spongy pedal: almost always air in the hydraulic system.
  • Sinking pedal under steady pressure: internal master cylinder leak past the seals.
Slide 3 of 3

Free Play and Why It Matters

Free play exists by design. Without it, the master cylinder primary cup can sit over the compensating port, blocking fluid return.

Too little free play: brakes may drag or self-apply as fluid heats and expands with nowhere to return.
Too much free play: excess pedal travel before braking starts. Often caused by worn pedal pivot or misadjusted pushrod.

Always check the service manual for the spec. Free play is usually a small number, often around a quarter inch.
