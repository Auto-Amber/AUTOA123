ABS, TCS & ESC: Shared Sensor Data

Three Systems, Same Sensors

ABS, traction control, and electronic stability control are often grouped together because they share hardware:

  • ABS (Antilock Brake System): Prevents wheel lockup under braking.
  • TCS (Traction Control System): Prevents wheel spin under acceleration.
  • ESC (Electronic Stability Control): Prevents understeer/oversteer by selectively braking individual wheels.
All three rely on the same four wheel speed sensors and the same HCU (with some additional valves in newer designs).
Slide 2 of 3

Different Goals, Same Inputs

What each system looks for in the wheel speed data:

  • ABS: A wheel decelerating significantly faster than vehicle speed (about to lock).
  • TCS: A driven wheel accelerating significantly faster than vehicle speed (spinning).
  • ESC: Wheel speed differences that suggest the vehicle is sliding sideways or not following its intended path. Often combined with yaw rate sensor and steering angle sensor data.

Same four wheels reporting, three different things being watched for, three different responses.

Slide 3 of 3

Why It Matters for Diagnosis

When a single wheel speed sensor fails:

  • ABS will set a DTC and disable.
  • TCS will set a DTC and disable.
  • ESC will set a DTC and disable.
One failed sensor takes down all three systems. A customer who complains "my traction control light is on" may actually have a wheel speed sensor failure that's also disabled ABS and ESC. Always scan all related modules.

Yaw rate sensor failures, on the other hand, typically only affect ESC, since ABS and TCS don't depend on yaw data.
