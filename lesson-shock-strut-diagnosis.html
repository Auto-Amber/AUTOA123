Symptoms of Worn Dampers

Shocks and struts wear gradually. Drivers often don't notice until they're severely worn. Watch for:

  • Excessive bouncing after going over bumps - 3+ bounces is a fail
  • Nose-dive under braking - front end drops dramatically
  • Body roll in corners worse than normal
  • Cupped tire wear - scallops around the tread caused by the tire skipping
  • Wandering at highway speeds
  • Wet streaks down the shock or strut body - seal failure

Any combination of these points to damper wear.

Tests You Can Do

Three practical tests:

  1. Bounce test - push down hard on a corner, release, count bounces. Should settle in 1-2 cycles. 3+ is fail.
  2. Visual inspection - look for oil weeping at the seal, dented or rusted body, damaged or missing bump stops.
  3. Road test - hit a known bumpy section and pay attention to how the vehicle settles. Compare front to rear.
Diagnostic tip

Cupped tire wear is one of the most reliable signs. If the tread shows scallops, the damper has been failing long enough that the tire was skipping off the road.

When to Replace

General guidelines for shock/strut replacement:

  • Any visible fluid leak - replace
  • Fails the bounce test - replace
  • Damaged body, bent shaft, or broken mount - replace
  • Vehicle has 100,000+ miles - inspect carefully even if no obvious failure
Always replace in pairs

Replace both front shocks together or both rear shocks together. Mixing a new shock with an old one creates uneven damping and unpredictable handling.

After replacement, always re-check wheel alignment.

Knowledge Check - Question 1 of 3
A vehicle bounces excessively after going over bumps, dives heavily under braking, and shows cupped tire wear. What is the MOST likely cause?
  • Worn ball joints
  • Bent steering knuckle
  • Worn shocks or struts
  • Loose lug nuts
Correct. Excessive bouncing, nose-dive under braking, AND cupped tire wear are the classic three symptoms of worn dampers. The damper has lost the ability to control spring motion.
Not quite. All three symptoms - bouncing, dive, cupped wear - point to the damper. Ball joints cause clunking. Bent knuckles cause uncorrectable alignment. Loose lug nuts cause vibration.
Knowledge Check - Question 2 of 3
What are FOUR symptoms of worn shock absorbers?
  • Bouncing, nose-dive under braking, cupped tire wear, wet streaks on the body
  • Tire feathering, brake pull, ABS warning light, low fuel economy
  • Steering wander, clunking on bumps, grinding when turning, brake noise
  • Low coolant, high RPM, hard shifting, dim headlights
Correct. Bouncing (3+ cycles), nose-dive under braking, cupped/scalloped tire wear, and oil leakage on the shock body - these four together are the textbook list.
Not quite. The textbook four are: excessive bouncing, nose-dive under braking, cupped tire wear, and oil leakage from the shock seal. The other answers mix in symptoms from unrelated systems.
Knowledge Check - Question 3 of 3
After replacing one front strut with a new one, you tell the customer the other side should also be replaced. Why?
  • Because the new strut has a 1-year warranty
  • Because uneven damping between sides creates unpredictable handling
  • Because the alignment can't be set otherwise
  • Because the wheel bearings will fail
Correct. Mixing a new strut with an old, worn one creates different damping rates side to side. The vehicle pulls under braking, handles unpredictably in corners, and tire wear becomes uneven.
Not quite. The reason to replace in pairs is matched damping. A new strut on one side and an old worn strut on the other = unbalanced suspension = poor handling and uneven tire wear.
