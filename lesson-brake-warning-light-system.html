Two Different Warning Lights

Modern vehicles use two different brake warning lights, and they mean different things:

  • Red BRAKE light: hydraulic system problem, parking brake engaged, or low brake fluid.
  • Yellow ABS light: ABS / TCS / ESC system fault. Base brakes still work normally.
If the red light is on and the parking brake is released, treat the vehicle as unsafe to drive until you find the cause.
Slide 2 of 3

What Triggers the Red Light

Three common triggers for the red BRAKE warning light:

  • Parking brake switch: simple switch on the pedal or lever. Closes when applied.
  • Low fluid switch: float-style switch in the master cylinder reservoir. Closes when fluid drops.
  • Pressure differential switch: mounted in the combination valve or proportioning valve. Detects unequal pressure between the front and rear hydraulic circuits.
Slide 3 of 3

The Pressure Differential Switch

The master cylinder has two separate circuits (primary and secondary) for a reason: if one circuit leaks, the other still stops the car.

The pressure differential switch sits between the two circuits. Under normal operation, pressure is equal and the switch stays open. If one side loses pressure, the spool inside the switch shifts toward the low-pressure side, closing the switch and lighting the red brake warning.

The light stays on even after the leak is fixed until you bleed the system and the spool re-centers. Don't condemn the switch until you've bled both circuits.
