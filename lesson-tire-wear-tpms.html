Reading Tire Wear

A tire tells you its history. Before any alignment, rotation, or suspension diagnosis, read the existing wear pattern on all four tires. The wear pattern often points directly to the cause before you touch an alignment machine.

Wear PatternWhat it looks likeLikely cause
Center wearWorn in the middle, good on edgesChronic overinflation
Edge wear (both edges)Worn on both outer edges, good in centerChronic underinflation
One-edge wear (inner)Rapid wear on inner edge onlyExcessive negative camber
One-edge wear (outer)Rapid wear on outer edge onlyExcessive positive camber
Feathered (saw-blade)Tread blocks worn on one side, sharp on other; feel it with your handIncorrect toe
Cupping/scallopingHigh and low spots around the circumferenceWorn shock/strut, out-of-balance tire
Flat spotsOne or more squared-off flat areasWheel lockup during panic stop or storage flat spot
Tread depth spec: Legal minimum is 2/32 in. Recommend replacement at 4/32 in (wet traction degrades significantly below this). A quarter coin inserted into the tread = 4/32 in at the top of Washington's head. A penny = 2/32 in.

Tire Rotation Patterns

Regular tire rotation equalizes wear across all four tires, extending overall tire life. The correct rotation pattern depends on whether tires are directional or non-directional and whether the vehicle is front-wheel, rear-wheel, or all-wheel drive.

Always look up the manufacturer's recommended rotation pattern in ShopKey first. Generic patterns do not account for all variables (staggered fitment, full-size spare inclusion, directional tires).

Common patterns:

  • Forward cross (FWD): Front tires go straight back. Rear tires cross to opposite front positions.
  • Rearward cross (RWD/AWD): Rear tires go straight forward. Front tires cross to opposite rear positions.
  • X-pattern: All four tires cross to opposite corners. Used on some FWD vehicles.
  • Directional tires: Can only move front-to-rear on the same side (rotation pattern is side-specific, not crossable). They have a one-way tread pattern indicated by an arrow on the sidewall.
  • Staggered fitment: If front and rear tires are different sizes, they cannot be rotated at all -- inform the customer.

TPMS -- Tire Pressure Monitoring System

Federal law has required TPMS on all new passenger vehicles since 2008. The system warns the driver when any tire pressure drops 25% or more below the vehicle's recommended inflation pressure.

Two system types:

  • Direct TPMS: A pressure sensor inside each wheel transmits individual pressure readings to the TPMS module. More accurate, but sensors can fail and have a battery life of approximately 5-10 years.
  • Indirect TPMS: Uses the ABS wheel speed sensors to detect a rolling-diameter change caused by an underinflated tire (it spins slightly faster). No sensors in the wheels, but cannot identify which tire is low or give a pressure reading.
TPMS service triggers:
-- Tire rotation: direct TPMS sensors must be re-trained to their new positions after rotation (scan tool or magnet procedure depending on system)
-- Tire replacement: if the old sensor is reused, inspect the valve core and grommet; replace the service kit (core, cap, nut, grommet)
-- Sensor replacement: requires programming the new sensor ID to the TPMS module
