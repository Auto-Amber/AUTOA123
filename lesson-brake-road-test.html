Brake System Road Test

Before You Drive

The road test is the most important diagnostic tool you have for the brake system. But preparation matters:

  • Verify brake fluid level. Don't road test with a low reservoir.
  • Check tire pressures (all four should match spec).
  • Do a stationary pedal test first. Pedal should be firm and high.
  • Confirm the parking brake holds and releases.
  • Walk around the vehicle. Listen for hissing (vacuum leak) with the engine running.
If anything fails the stationary checks, don't road test until it's resolved.
Slide 2 of 3

On the Road

A complete brake road test includes:

  • Light braking from low speed: Listen for noise, feel for vibration, check for pull.
  • Moderate braking from 30-40 mph: Verify smooth pedal, even braking, no pulsation or pull.
  • Firm braking from 40-50 mph on dry pavement: Confirm strong stopping power without lockup.
  • ABS function test: On safe, empty wet or gravel surface, apply hard braking to verify ABS activates. Pedal will pulse, motor will buzz. This is normal.
  • Parking brake test: Apply on a slight grade and verify it holds the vehicle.
Always have a clear road behind you for hard braking tests. Brake-checking the car behind you is dangerous.
Slide 3 of 3

What to Document

Note any abnormal behavior on the repair order:

  • Pulling direction (left or right) and at what severity
  • Pedal feel (spongy, low, hard, normal)
  • Noise (squeal, grind, click) and when it occurs
  • Vibration and at what speeds
  • Warning lights that came on during the test
  • ABS activation success or failure

Documentation protects you legally and gives the next technician (or yourself, in two weeks) a clear picture of the symptoms.
