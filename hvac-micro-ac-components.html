Moving Heat

The condenser is on the high side. It rejects heat and changes high pressure gas to high pressure liquid, and runs very hot at its inlet.
The evaporator is on the low side. It absorbs cabin heat and changes low pressure liquid to low pressure gas, and runs cold.
Together they move heat from inside the cabin to the outside air.
Slide 2 of 3

Storage and Drying

A receiver drier is used on TXV systems, sits between the condenser and evaporator, and holds high pressure liquid. It stores liquid, filters, and dries with a desiccant.
An accumulator is used on orifice tube systems, sits between the evaporator and compressor, and holds low pressure gas.
The accumulator keeps liquid refrigerant out of the compressor and also dries with a desiccant.
Replace the drier or accumulator when the system has been open or a compressor has failed.
Slide 3 of 3

The Compressor and Oil

The compressor pumps and pressurizes the refrigerant, turning low pressure gas into high pressure gas.
Most use an electromagnetic clutch and are protected by low and high pressure switches.
Oil circulates with the refrigerant and is the only lubrication the compressor gets. R134a systems use PAG oil, and subtypes matter.
Knowledge Check 1 of 3

Which component keeps liquid refrigerant out of the compressor on orifice tube systems?

Answer: Accumulator. The accumulator sits on the low side and stops liquid from reaching the compressor.
Knowledge Check 2 of 3

What protects the compressor by opening the circuit when pressure gets too high?

Answer: High pressure switch. The high pressure switch opens the circuit, typically when pressure climbs over about 375 psi.
Knowledge Check 3 of 3

What lubricates the compressor?

Answer: The oil that circulates with the refrigerant. Oil travels with the refrigerant and is the compressor's only source of lubrication. R134a uses PAG oil.
