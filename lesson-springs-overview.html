The Job of a Spring

Every suspension system uses a spring of some kind. The spring's job is simple but critical:

  • Support the weight of the vehicle
  • Absorb energy when the wheel moves up
  • Release that energy slowly so the wheel can return
  • Allow the wheel to follow road surface changes

Without a spring, every bump would transfer directly into the vehicle frame and the passengers. The springs are what let the wheels move independently while the body stays relatively stable.

Slide 2 of 3
Types You'll See

Four spring types cover almost everything you'll work on:

  • Coil springs - most common. Round helical steel. Used on most cars, light trucks.
  • Leaf springs - stacked flat steel leaves. Common on truck rear axles.
  • Torsion bars - steel rod that twists. Older trucks and SUVs.
  • Air springs - rubber bladder filled with compressed air. Luxury vehicles, heavy trucks.

Different shapes, same job. The vehicle's intended use determines which type the engineer specified.

Slide 3 of 3
Spring vs Shock

Students often confuse springs and shocks. They work together but do different jobs.

The difference

The spring stores energy. The shock absorber dampens the release of that energy so the spring doesn't keep bouncing forever.

If you removed the shock, the spring would keep oscillating after every bump - the car would bounce for ten seconds after hitting a pothole.

If you removed the spring, there would be nothing to support the vehicle weight - the chassis would sit on the bump stops.

Both must work. Both must be checked.

Knowledge Check - Question 1 of 3
All suspension types use ________ of one form or another.
  • Beam axles
  • Control arms
  • Pivot shafts
  • Springs
Correct. Every suspension uses some form of spring - coil, leaf, torsion bar, or air - to support the vehicle weight and absorb road impacts.
Not quite. The universal component is the spring. Control arms, beam axles, and pivot shafts are specific to certain designs.
Knowledge Check - Question 2 of 3
What is the difference between a spring and a shock absorber?
  • Springs are bigger than shocks
  • Springs store energy; shocks dampen the release of that energy
  • Shocks are stronger than springs
  • Springs are mounted in front, shocks in the rear
Correct. The spring stores energy when compressed. The shock controls how fast that energy releases, preventing the spring from oscillating forever.
Not quite. Size and location aren't what define them. The functional difference: spring stores energy, shock dampens its release. Both work together.
Knowledge Check - Question 3 of 3
Which spring type is most commonly found on a truck's rear axle?
  • Coil
  • Leaf
  • Torsion bar
  • Air
Correct. Leaf springs are still standard on truck rear axles because they handle high loads, locate the axle, and resist twist all at once.
Not quite. Leaf springs dominate truck rear axles. Coil and torsion bars are common in passenger cars and SUVs; air springs are mostly luxury and heavy-duty.
