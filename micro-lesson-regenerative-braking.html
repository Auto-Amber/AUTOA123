Regenerative Braking

The Basic Idea

In a hybrid or EV, the drive motor can run in reverse as a generator. When the driver releases the throttle or applies the brake, the motor becomes a generator:

  • The vehicle's kinetic energy spins the motor.
  • The motor produces electrical energy.
  • That energy is stored in the high-voltage battery for later use.
  • The braking force on the motor slows the vehicle.
This is "regenerative braking" because energy is regenerated (recovered) rather than turned to waste heat by friction brakes.
Slide 2 of 3

Blended Braking

Regenerative braking alone can't do everything:

  • The motor can only slow the driven wheels (rear in some designs, front in others, all in AWD).
  • Maximum regenerative braking force is limited by the motor and battery capacity.
  • At very low speeds, regenerative braking force drops off.
  • Hard braking demands more force than regen can provide.
The vehicle "blends" regenerative and friction braking automatically. The brake module decides moment by moment how much braking comes from regen and how much from the friction brakes. The driver feels normal pedal feel throughout.
Slide 3 of 3

When Friction Takes Over

Friction brakes always take over in these cases:

  • Battery is full: No room to store regenerated energy.
  • Battery is very cold: Lithium chemistry doesn't accept charge well below freezing.
  • Hard or panic braking: More force needed than regen can deliver.
  • Very low speed: Motor can't generate at near-zero RPM.
  • ABS event: Regen is disabled during ABS activation.
This is why hybrid and EV brake pads can last 100,000+ miles. Most stops are done by regen, the friction brakes only step in occasionally.
