Why Brake Balance Matters

During braking, vehicle weight shifts forward — loading the front tires and unloading the rear tires. The harder the stop, the more weight transfers forward.

This creates a serious problem: if the rear brakes receive the same hydraulic pressure as the front brakes, the lightly loaded rear tires will lock up first — causing the rear of the vehicle to swing out (oversteer, spinout).

The proportioning valve reduces hydraulic pressure to the rear brakes above a certain threshold. At low pedal pressures, front and rear get equal pressure. As pressure rises above the split point, rear pressure increases at a reduced rate.

This matches rear brake force to the actual load on the rear tires — maximizing brake effectiveness without causing rear lockup.

Without a proportioning valve (or with a failed one that is stuck open), the rear brakes will lock before the fronts in moderate-to-hard stops.

Types of Proportioning Valves

Three main designs:

Fixed Proportioning Valve
Built into the combination valve or master cylinder. One fixed split point. Simple and reliable. Most common on older vehicles.
Load-Sensing Proportioning Valve (LSPV)
Adjusts split point based on vehicle load. Connected mechanically to the rear suspension. More rear brake when loaded, less when empty. Trucks and SUVs.
Electronic Brake Force Distribution (EBD)
Software function of the ABS module. Uses wheel speed sensors to detect impending rear lockup and reduces rear pressure via ABS solenoids. Modern vehicles.
On vehicles with EBD (electronic brake force distribution), a failed ABS system also disables the proportioning function. An ABS fault on these vehicles means both ABS AND brake balance are compromised. Treat as a high-priority repair.

The combination valve on older vehicles combined the metering valve, proportioning valve, and a pressure differential switch into one assembly.

Proportioning Valve Diagnosis

Symptoms of a failed proportioning valve:

Failure ModeSymptomRisk
Stuck open (passes full pressure)Rear wheels lock before fronts in moderate stopsRear of vehicle swings out — spinout risk
Stuck closed (passes no pressure)Only front brakes working — excessive pedal travel, reduced brakingReduced stopping power
LSPV linkage brokenRear bias does not change with loadRear lockup when unloaded

Testing:

  • Road test: during a moderate-to-firm stop, rear wheels should not lock before fronts
  • Pressure gauge: measure front and rear caliper pressure simultaneously — rear should be lower than front above the split point
  • LSPV: verify linkage is connected and moves freely with suspension travel
On vehicles with EBD, the ABS scan tool live data shows individual wheel brake pressure. Use this to verify rear pressure reduction is occurring during threshold braking.
