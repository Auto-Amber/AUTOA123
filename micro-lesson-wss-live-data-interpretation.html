Four Wheels, Four Numbers

When you connect a scan tool to a modern vehicle, you can pull up live data PIDs that show the speed reported by each wheel sensor:

  • LF (Left Front)
  • RF (Right Front)
  • LR (Left Rear)
  • RR (Right Rear)
On a vehicle driving in a straight line on dry pavement, all four wheel speeds should read within 1-2 MPH of each other.
Slide 2 of 3

Spotting the Odd Wheel

If one wheel is reporting differently from the others:

  • Reading zero when the others show speed: Sensor signal is being lost. Could be wiring, connector, sensor itself, or tone ring missing teeth.
  • Erratic / jumpy reading: Air gap issue (loose bearing, debris between sensor and tone ring), or wiring problem under load.
  • Always slightly off: Could be a different tire size than the others (mismatched tires, mini spare installed), or tone ring with a slight inconsistency.
  • Reads correctly at low speed but drops out at high speed: Sometimes a passive sensor with weak signal due to air gap or coil resistance issue.
Slide 3 of 3

Test Drive with Scan Tool Recording

For intermittent issues, use the scan tool's recording function:

  • Set up the scan tool to record all four wheel speed PIDs.
  • Take the vehicle for a drive that replicates the customer's complaint conditions (specific speed range, turns, road surface, etc.).
  • Mark the time when the DTC sets or the warning light comes on.
  • Play back the recording and look at all four wheel speeds at that moment.
The wheel that drops out, jumps, or reads erratically is your problem wheel. From there, you check that wheel's sensor, wiring, connector, tone ring, and bearing.
