Four Bleeding Methods

Air in a hydraulic system makes the pedal spongy. To get the air out, you have four options:

  • Manual (two-person) bleeding: one person pumps the pedal, the other opens and closes the bleeder.
  • Pressure bleeding: a pressurized tank pushes fluid through the system from the reservoir.
  • Vacuum bleeding: a vacuum pump pulls fluid and air out through the bleeder.
  • Gravity bleeding: open the bleeders and let fluid drip out slowly. Slow but works for simple systems.
Slide 2 of 3

Bleeding Sequence

Always follow the manufacturer's sequence. The traditional rule was longest-line-first (RR, LR, RF, LF on most rear-wheel drives), but modern vehicles often have specific sequences in the service manual.

Always reference the service manual. Diagonally split systems, ABS-equipped vehicles, and hybrids all have specific procedures.

Top off the reservoir often. If the reservoir runs dry mid-bleed, you'll suck more air into the master cylinder and have to start over.

Slide 3 of 3

When You Need a Scan Tool

Many modern ABS systems have isolation valves, dump valves, and accumulators that hold air pockets that you can't reach by bleeding at the wheel cylinders alone.

You need a scan tool for ABS bleeding when:
  • You opened any ABS hydraulic line
  • You replaced the master cylinder, ABS module, or HCU
  • The pedal stays spongy after normal bleeding at all four wheels

The scan tool runs an active command to cycle the ABS solenoids and pump, pushing air out of pockets that normal bleeding can't reach.
