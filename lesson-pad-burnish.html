Brake Pad Burnish & Break-In

What Burnish Means

Burnish (also called bedding-in) is a specific break-in procedure for new brake pads. It involves a series of controlled stops to transfer a thin, even layer of friction material onto the rotor surface.

The goal: a uniform transfer layer of friction material across the rotor face. This is what gives consistent, quiet, predictable braking.

Without burnishing, the transfer layer ends up patchy. Patchy transfer causes pulsation, noise, and inconsistent pedal feel.

Slide 2 of 3

Typical Procedure

Most manufacturers' burnish procedures look similar:

  • Find a safe, empty road where you can do repeated stops without traffic.
  • Make 6-10 moderate stops from about 30-40 mph down to about 10 mph.
  • Don't come to a complete stop. Don't hold the brake pedal at low speed.
  • Allow the brakes to cool between stops. Don't let them heat soak.
  • After the cycle, drive normally for a few miles to let the brakes fully cool.
Always check the pad manufacturer's specific recommendation. High-performance and ceramic compounds may require different procedures.
Slide 3 of 3

What Happens If You Skip It

Skip the burnish and you might see:

  • Pedal pulsation: uneven transfer layer creates effective thickness variation in the rotor.
  • Brake noise: squealing, groaning, or rattling.
  • Reduced stopping power: until the transfer layer is fully developed, the pads don't perform at their rated friction coefficient.
  • Premature pad wear: patchy contact accelerates wear on the high spots.
This is why "vehicle returned with brake pulsation" after a brake job often isn't a warped rotor. It's improper bedding.
