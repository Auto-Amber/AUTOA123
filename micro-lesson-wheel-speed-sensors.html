Wheel Speed Sensors: Passive vs Active
Progress · 0%
Slide 1 of 3

Two Sensor Types

Wheel speed sensors come in two flavors:

  • Passive (AC generator): A magnet inside a coil. As the toothed tone ring spins past, it induces an AC voltage signal that varies in frequency and amplitude with speed.
  • Active (Hall effect or magneto-resistive): A powered sensor that outputs a clean digital square wave. Frequency increases with speed but voltage stays constant.
Active sensors work at very low speeds (down to nearly zero). Passive sensors lose their signal at very low speeds.
Slide 2 of 3

Testing a Passive Sensor

A passive sensor is just a coil. Test it with a DVOM:

  • Resistance: Set DVOM to ohms. Should fall in the spec range (often a few hundred to several thousand ohms).
  • AC voltage output: Spin the wheel by hand and watch the DVOM in AC volts. Should see a voltage that rises with speed.
  • Air gap: Distance between sensor tip and tone ring. Too far = no signal.
Slide 3 of 3

Testing an Active Sensor

Active sensors need power. Don't test them like passive sensors.

  • Power and ground: Check with key on. Sensor should have battery voltage (or sometimes 5V or 8V) on the power wire and a good ground.
  • Live data on scan tool: The easiest test. Spin the wheel and watch wheel speed go up in the scan tool live data PIDs.
  • Scope pattern: A scope will show the digital square wave. Frequency increases with speed.
Common mistake: Trying to test an active sensor with the DVOM in resistance mode. That will damage the sensor. Always know which type you have before testing.
