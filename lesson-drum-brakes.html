Drum Brake Components and Operation

Drum brakes use curved brake shoes lined with friction material that press outward against the inside of a rotating brake drum. The drum rotates with the wheel hub.

Key components:

  • Brake drum: Cast iron, rotates with wheel. Inside diameter is the friction surface.
  • Brake shoes (primary and secondary): Curved steel backing with friction lining bonded or riveted on.
  • Wheel cylinder: Hydraulic cylinder with two pistons that push the tops of both shoes outward.
  • Return springs: Pull shoes back off the drum when brakes are released.
  • Hold-down hardware: Pins and springs that keep shoes positioned on the backing plate.
  • Adjuster: Star wheel adjuster maintains correct shoe-to-drum clearance as shoes wear.
The primary (front) shoe and secondary (rear) shoe are NOT the same. They are different lengths and the friction lining may be different. Never swap them.

Self-Energizing Action

Drum brakes have a unique characteristic called self-energizing (or servo) action. The design causes the rotating drum to pull the brake shoe more tightly into the drum as braking force increases — the brake partially applies itself.

How it works (forward rotation, duo-servo design):

  1. Wheel cylinder pushes primary shoe into the drum
  2. Drum rotation drags the primary shoe forward (in direction of travel)
  3. Primary shoe force transfers through the adjuster to the secondary shoe
  4. Secondary shoe is driven hard into the drum by this transferred force
  5. Secondary shoe does most of the braking work in forward direction
Self-energizing makes drum brakes very effective for parking brakes but more prone to grabbing or pulling if components are worn, contaminated, or improperly adjusted. Even a small amount of grease contamination causes severe brake grab.

Drum Brake Service

Key measurements:

MeasurementToolAction
Drum inside diameterDrum micrometer or inside micMachine if within discard limit; replace if at/beyond discard
Shoe lining thicknessRuler or vernier caliperReplace at 1/16" (1.5mm) remaining lining above rivet
Wheel cylinder for leaksVisual / boot inspectionReplace if any fluid present behind boot

Common drum brake problems:

  • Brake grab/pull: Contaminated lining (grease or fluid), misadjusted shoes, seized adjuster
  • Brake squeal: Glazed drum or lining, worn lining, hardware resonance
  • Poor adjustment: Excessive pedal travel, requires multiple pumps to firm up pedal
  • Brake drag: Seized wheel cylinder, weak return springs, over-adjusted shoes
Always replace drum brake hardware (springs, hold-downs, adjuster) as a complete kit when replacing shoes. Old springs weaken over time and cause premature wear and noise.
