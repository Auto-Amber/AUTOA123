ABS Warning Lamps
Progress · 0%
Slide 1 of 3

Two Different Lamps

Modern brake systems use two separate warning lamps in the dash:

  • Yellow ABS lamp: A fault in the ABS, TCS, or ESC system. The base brakes still work. The vehicle is drivable, but ABS is disabled.
  • Red BRAKE lamp: A serious problem with the base hydraulic brake system. Treat as unsafe to drive until diagnosed.
Both lamps on at the same time usually means a major fault has caused the ABS module to disable itself AND triggered a hydraulic warning. Always treat as unsafe.
Slide 2 of 3

Bulb Check at Startup

When you turn the key to RUN (engine off), both warning lamps should illuminate briefly as a bulb check. This confirms:

  • The bulb itself is good (not burned out).
  • The module is alive and reporting status to the cluster.

After the engine starts and the system completes its self-check (a few seconds), both lamps should go out.

If a lamp doesn't come on during the bulb check, the bulb is burned out or the module isn't communicating. This is a problem in itself; the driver has no way to know if ABS later fails.
Slide 3 of 3

Lamp Stays On After Startup

If a warning lamp stays on after the startup self-check completes:

  • Yellow ABS lamp: there's a stored DTC. Use a scan tool to read codes from the ABS module specifically (not just the engine module).
  • Red BRAKE lamp: check parking brake first, then fluid level, then look for evidence of a hydraulic fault.
A scan tool that only reads engine DTCs will miss ABS codes. Make sure your scan tool accesses the ABS module directly.
