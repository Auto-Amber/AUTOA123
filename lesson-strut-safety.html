Strut Spring Compressor Safety
Stored energy can kill - how to do it right

The Danger

A coil spring on a strut stores enough energy to be fatal if released suddenly. A typical strut spring holds 40+ joules of stored energy under load.

If a spring releases unexpectedly:

  • It can launch with enough velocity to penetrate the human body
  • It can reach speeds that exceed bullet velocities at close range
  • Even a "lucky" miss often damages walls, equipment, and other tools
Real consequence

Search "strut spring injury" and you'll find documented fatalities and severe injuries. This is not theoretical. There is no second chance.

Slide 2 of 3
Before You Start

Required steps every time you compress a strut spring:

  • Inspect the compressor - no cracks, no bent hooks, no missing parts
  • Verify the rating - the compressor must be rated for the spring you're working on
  • Use a strut cage when available - safer than open-style compressors
  • Seat the hooks fully on spring coils - never partial
  • Compress evenly - alternate sides so the spring doesn't skew or pop out
Stop and ask

If anything doesn't look right, STOP. Ask your instructor. There are no points lost for asking. There are points lost - and worse - for guessing wrong on safety.

Slide 3 of 3
Never Do These

Things that will get you hurt or killed:

  • Never disassemble a strut without compressing the spring first
  • Never use a damaged or wrong-size compressor
  • Never compress one side without alternating - the spring will skew and could shoot out
  • Never stand in line with the spring's axis during compression
  • Never walk away from a compressed spring - even for a minute
Procedure for removal

Compress until the spring is loose at the top mount. Confirm it's loose by hand-rotating. Only then remove the top nut. After the strut is apart, store the compressed spring in a safe place.

Knowledge Check - Question 1 of 3
Before the strut insert (cartridge) can be removed from a typical MacPherson strut assembly, what operation is necessary to prevent personal injury?
  • Remove the brake caliper and hose from the strut housing
  • Compress the coil spring
  • Remove the upper strut mounting bolts
  • Remove the lower attaching bolts
Correct. The coil spring stores enough energy to kill. Always compress the spring before removing the top nut or attempting any strut disassembly. The other steps don't address the stored spring energy.
Not quite. The danger is stored spring energy. Compressing the spring relieves the load on the top nut so the strut can be safely disassembled. Removing mounting bolts doesn't reduce the spring's stored energy.
Knowledge Check - Question 2 of 3
Which of these is a SAFE practice when using a strut spring compressor?
  • Compressing one side fully before compressing the other side
  • Using a compressor rated for a smaller spring if it physically fits
  • Verifying the compressor is rated for the spring, seating hooks fully, and alternating sides
  • Standing in line with the spring axis to watch for slipping
Correct. Verify the rating, seat the hooks fully, alternate sides. That's the safe practice. Each step prevents a specific failure mode.
Not quite. Safe practice is: rated compressor, fully seated hooks, alternating sides. Compressing one side fully first skews the spring. Wrong-rated compressors fail. Standing in the line of fire is exactly what you don't do.
Knowledge Check - Question 3 of 3
You compressed the spring but can't tell if it's loose enough to safely remove the top nut. What should you do?
  • Try to remove the nut and see what happens
  • Tap the spring with a hammer to test
  • Compress further until you can rotate the spring by hand at the top mount
  • Loosen the nut slowly while staying close
Correct. If you can rotate the spring at the top mount by hand, it's loose - the spring is no longer pushing against the top mount. Only then is it safe to remove the top nut.
Not quite. The test is: can you rotate the spring by hand at the top mount? If yes, it's loose enough. If no, compress more. Guessing, hammering, or 'trying' the nut are all unsafe. Always verify the spring is fully relieved.
