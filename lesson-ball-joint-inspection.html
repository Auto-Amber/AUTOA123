Two Types of Ball Joints

Ball joints come in two main types based on how you check them:

  • Indicator-type (wear indicator) - has a visible "tell" built into the joint. Often a grease fitting that recedes as the joint wears.
  • Non-indicator type - no visible indicator. You must measure play with a dial indicator or pry bar.

The inspection method is completely different for each type. Using the wrong method gives wrong results.

Slide 2 of 3
Indicator-Type Inspection

For indicator ball joints, the manufacturer designed the joint so wear is visible WHILE the joint is loaded by vehicle weight.

Indicator method

Vehicle on the ground (loaded). Inspect the grease fitting or the indicator. If it has receded into the housing, the joint is worn out.

This is fast and easy. No measurement needed - it's a pass/fail visual check.

Some indicators are a wear groove on the boot side. Some are dimensions you can check with a depth gauge. Always reference the service manual procedure for the specific vehicle.

Slide 3 of 3
Non-Indicator Inspection

For non-indicator joints, you must measure the actual play in the joint - and the joint must be UNLOADED to measure it accurately.

Non-indicator method

Vehicle on a hoist. If the joint is load-carrying, place a jack stand under the lower control arm (close to the joint) to UNLOAD it. Then pry the wheel up and measure play with a dial indicator.

Typical specs: less than 0.050 inch of vertical play. Check the service manual for your vehicle.

Critical

Loading vs unloading: indicator = LOADED. Non-indicator = UNLOADED. Mixing these up gives false readings every time.

Knowledge Check - Question 1 of 3
Two technicians are discussing non-indicator-type ball joint inspection. Tech A says the vehicle should be on the ground with the ball joints LOADED, then checked for free play. Tech B says the ball joints should be UNLOADED before checking for free play. Who is correct?
  • Tech A only
  • Tech B only
  • Both technicians
  • Neither technician
Correct. Tech B is correct. Non-indicator joints must be UNLOADED to measure play. Tech A's procedure (loaded) is for INDICATOR-type joints, not non-indicator. The two statements describe opposite methods, so only one can apply to non-indicator joints.
Not quite. Tech B is right. Non-indicator joints need to be unloaded so you can measure the play. Tech A described the loaded method, which is for INDICATOR-type joints. These two methods are opposites and only Tech B's applies here.
Knowledge Check - Question 2 of 3
How do you determine if a ball joint with a wear indicator is worn out?
  • Pry the joint with a bar and look for movement
  • Measure the joint with a micrometer
  • Inspect the indicator (grease fitting or wear line) with the vehicle loaded
  • Listen for clunking sounds
Correct. Indicator-type joints are designed for visual check while LOADED. The grease fitting or wear indicator will be receded if the joint is worn beyond spec.
Not quite. Indicator joints have a designed-in visual cue. Check it while the vehicle weight is on the joint (loaded). Prying and measuring are for NON-indicator types. Sound is helpful but not the official method.
Knowledge Check - Question 3 of 3
Why must a non-indicator ball joint be unloaded before checking for play?
  • Because the joint is too hot to touch when loaded
  • Because the vehicle weight holds the joint tight, hiding the actual play
  • Because the boot will tear if loaded
  • Non-indicator joints are checked loaded, not unloaded
Correct. Vehicle weight on a load-carrying ball joint clamps it tight - any play is masked. Unloading the joint (jack stand under the LCA) lets the actual play show up when you pry.
Not quite. Vehicle weight masks the play. The joint has to be unloaded so the slack can be felt. Place a jack stand under the lower control arm to take the load off, then check.
