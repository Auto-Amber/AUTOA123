Brake Pad Wear Patterns & Diagnosis
Progress · 0%
Slide 1 of 3

Normal vs Abnormal Wear

A worn-out pad isn't necessarily a worn-out pad. The wear PATTERN is what tells you whether the system is healthy:

  • Even wear, both pads matched: normal. Caliper is doing its job.
  • Inner pad worn more than outer: the piston side is the one pushing. Slides may be binding (outer pad isn't being pulled in).
  • Outer pad worn more than inner: piston may be sticking, but the caliper is still floating against the outer pad.
  • One wheel's pads worn faster than the other side: hydraulic imbalance or one-sided caliper issue.
Slide 2 of 3

Tapered Wear

Tapered wear means the pad is thicker on one end than the other. Causes:

Inboard-outboard taper (one edge of the pad shorter): the rotor is not square to the caliper. Check for hub damage or bracket misalignment.
Top-bottom taper: one slide pin is binding, allowing the caliper to twist as it engages.

Tapered wear means parts are going to fail faster. Don't just replace the pads, find the cause.

Slide 3 of 3

Glazing and Cracking

Two surface conditions to watch for:

  • Glazed pads: hard, shiny surface from overheating. Pads bake and harden, friction drops. Often from continuous light braking on a long downhill.
  • Cracked pads: visible cracks across the friction surface. The pad has been overheated past its limit. Replace immediately.
Glazed or cracked pads point to either a sticking caliper, a hydraulic issue (residual pressure), or driver behavior. Investigate before just swapping pads.
