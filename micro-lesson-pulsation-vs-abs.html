Pulsation vs ABS Activation
Progress · 0%
Slide 1 of 3

Two Things That Feel Similar

Both warped rotors and ABS activation produce a pulsing brake pedal. The driver can't always tell which they're feeling, so YOU need to know:

  • Warped rotor pulsation: Pedal pulses with rotor rotation. Faster pulse at higher speeds.
  • ABS activation: Pedal pulses very rapidly (many times per second) and only during hard braking.
Pulse rate is the giveaway. Warped rotors give you maybe 5-10 pulses per second at highway speed. ABS gives you 15+ pulses per second, much faster.
Slide 2 of 3

When Each Happens

The conditions of pulsing tell you what's going on:

  • Warped rotor: Happens during normal braking, especially at moderate to high speeds. Worse on long downhills. Always there.
  • ABS: Only happens during hard braking, panic stops, or braking on slick surfaces. Goes away as soon as you release the pedal or the ABS event ends.
If a customer describes pulsation only when braking hard on wet roads, that's almost certainly ABS, not a brake problem. Educate them: keep firm pressure and let it work.
Slide 3 of 3

Confirming It

If you're not sure, here's how to confirm:

  • Test drive with moderate braking on dry pavement: If pulsation is present here, it's the brake system itself (warped rotor, uneven pad transfer).
  • Test drive with hard braking on dry pavement: If pulsation appears only here and the rear wheels stay locked momentarily, ABS is activating.
  • Listen for the pump motor: ABS makes a distinctive buzzing or grinding noise from the HCU during activation. Warped rotors don't.
  • Measure the rotors: If thickness variation and lateral runout are both within spec, the rotor isn't warped. Look elsewhere.
