Heat Moves -- It Does Not Disappear

A vehicle's A/C system does not create cold air. It moves heat. Understanding this one idea explains everything else in the system.

The core principle: Heat always flows from a warmer area to a cooler one. The refrigeration cycle exploits this by using a refrigerant that can absorb heat in one place and release it in another.

The refrigerant does this by changing state -- from liquid to vapor and back again. When a liquid evaporates, it absorbs a large amount of heat from its surroundings. When a vapor condenses, it releases that heat. This is called latent heat of vaporization.

Everyday example: When you sweat, the evaporation of moisture pulls heat out of your skin -- you feel cooler even though no heat was destroyed. The A/C evaporator works the same way, just with refrigerant instead of sweat.

The Four Stages of the Cycle

The refrigerant travels through four components in sequence, changing state and pressure twice along the way.

High side (compressor outlet to metering device): high pressure, hot. Low side (metering device to compressor inlet): low pressure, cold. The metering device is the dividing line between high and low side.

What Happens at Each Component

CompressorLow-pressure vapor is compressed into high-pressure, high-temp vaporHeat added by compression

Metering deviceHigh-pressure liquid drops to low pressure; some flashes to vapor (TXV or orifice tube)No heat transfer -- pressure drops
EvaporatorLow-pressure liquid absorbs heat from cabin air and boils into vaporHeat enters the system here
Remember: The evaporator absorbs heat from the cabin (makes you feel cool). The condenser dumps that heat outside the car. The compressor keeps the refrigerant moving and pressurized so the cycle can repeat.
