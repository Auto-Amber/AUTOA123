Disc Brake System Overview

Disc brakes use a rotor (disc) that rotates with the wheel, and a caliper that clamps brake pads against both sides of the rotor to create friction and slow the vehicle.

Key advantages over drum brakes:

  • Better heat dissipation: Rotor is exposed to airflow — cools faster
  • More consistent performance: Less prone to fade under repeated hard stops
  • Self-adjusting: As pads wear, caliper pistons extend automatically
  • Wet weather recovery: Rotor surfaces wipe dry on first application

Rotor construction types:

Solid rotorSingle solid discRear, light-duty applications
Vented rotorTwo faces with cooling vanes betweenFront brakes, heavy-duty

Caliper Types and Operation

The caliper is the hydraulic clamping device. Two main designs:

Fixed Caliper
Bolted rigidly to the knuckle. Has pistons on BOTH sides of the rotor. More expensive, better performance. Common on sports cars. Does not slide.
Floating (Sliding) Caliper
Slides on guide pins. Pistons on ONE side only — the caliper body floats to clamp both pads. Most common design. Requires clean, lubricated guide pins.

How the floating caliper works:

  1. Hydraulic pressure enters the caliper, pushing the piston outward
  2. Piston pushes the inner pad against the rotor
  3. Reaction force slides the caliper body inward on the guide pins
  4. Outer pad is pulled against the rotor by the caliper sliding
Seized guide pins prevent the caliper from floating — causing uneven pad wear, excessive heat on one side, and brake pull. Inspect and lubricate guide pins during every pad replacement.

Rotor Service and Pad Wear

Rotor inspection measurements:

MeasurementToolSpec Source
Rotor thicknessOutside micrometerShopKey — minimum thickness cast into rotor
Lateral runout (wobble)Dial indicatorTypically 0.002"–0.005" max
Parallelism (thickness variation)Outside micrometerTypically 0.0005"–0.001" max

Brake pad wear indicators:

  • Wear indicator tab: Metal tab that contacts rotor when pads are worn — produces a high-pitched squeal
  • Visual inspection: Measure pad thickness — typically replace at 2–3mm
  • Metal-to-metal grinding: Pad completely worn through — rotor damage occurring
Never machine a rotor below minimum thickness. An under-thickness rotor can crack or fail under load, causing sudden and complete loss of braking at that corner.
