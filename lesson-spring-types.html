Coil, leaf, and torsion bar specifics for inspection

Coil Springs

Coil springs are the most common spring type on modern vehicles. A length of round steel wound into a helix.

  • Used with shock absorbers, struts, and on all four corners of most cars
  • Spring isolators (rubber pads) at top and bottom prevent metal-on-metal contact
  • Failure modes: broken coils, sag (lost free length), missing or torn isolators
Inspection

Look for a broken coil (often the bottom one), missing isolators causing knocking sounds, and ride height that doesn't match the other corner.

Leaf Springs

Leaf springs are stacked flat steel strips bound together. They both support the vehicle AND locate the axle.

The center bolt is critical - it has two jobs:

  • Holds the individual leaves together as a stack
  • Locates the axle on the spring (the bolt head sits in a hole in the axle pad)
If the center bolt fails

The axle can shift forward or backward on the spring - changes pinion angle, can damage the driveshaft, and creates a violent pull when accelerating.

Also inspect: shackles, bushings, U-bolts, spring eye, and silencers (silencing pads between leaves).

Slide 3 of 3
Torsion Bars

A torsion bar is a steel rod anchored at one end and connected to a suspension arm at the other. When the suspension moves, the bar twists - that twist is the spring action.

Two things you must know:

  • Adjustable for ride height - most have an adjuster bolt at the anchor end. This is how you set or correct ride height on torsion bar vehicles.
  • Marked LEFT and RIGHT - factory pre-twisted in one direction. Never swap side to side, or the bar will work against itself.
Safety

Torsion bars hold massive stored energy. Never remove a torsion bar adjuster without first relieving the load using a torsion bar unloader tool.

Knowledge Check - Question 1 of 3
What two jobs does the center bolt do on a leaf spring?
  • Holds leaves together and aligns the shock mount
  • Holds leaves together and locates the axle on the spring
  • Locates the axle and provides ride height adjustment
  • Holds the shackle and aligns the U-bolts
Correct. The center bolt clamps all the individual leaves into one stack AND locates the axle on the spring through a pin that fits into the axle pad. If it shears, the axle can shift.
Not quite. Two jobs: bolt the leaves together as a stack, and locate the axle on top of the spring. U-bolts hold the axle DOWN onto the spring, but the center bolt locates it side-to-side and front-to-back.
Knowledge Check - Question 2 of 3
Two technicians are discussing torsion bars. Tech A says many torsion bars are adjustable to set ride height. Tech B says torsion bars are marked left/right and should not be swapped. Who is correct?
  • Tech A only
  • Tech B only
  • Both technicians
  • Neither technician
Correct. Both are correct. Torsion bars typically have an adjuster bolt for ride height, AND they are factory pre-twisted in one direction so they must stay on their original side.
Not quite. Both statements are true about torsion bars. They ARE adjustable for ride height, and they ARE side-specific because of the pre-twist. This is a classic two-technician question where both right answers address different attributes.
Knowledge Check - Question 3 of 3
Which inspection finding indicates a failed coil spring?
  • Slight surface rust on the coils
  • One coil is broken at the end, or the corner sits noticeably low
  • The spring isolators are visible from the wheel well
  • The spring is painted a different color than the others
Correct. A broken coil (usually one of the bottom coils that takes the most stress) and uneven ride height are the two main symptoms of a failed coil spring.
Not quite. Surface rust is normal. Visible isolators are normal. Color doesn't indicate failure. A broken coil or sagging ride height are the actual fail conditions.
