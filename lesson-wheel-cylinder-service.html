What the Wheel Cylinder Does

The wheel cylinder is the hydraulic actuator for a drum brake. When brake fluid pressure arrives, the two cups inside the cylinder push outward, spreading the brake shoes against the drum.

Two pistons, two cup seals, one spring in between. When pressure is released, return springs on the shoes pull them back, and the cups retract.

Modern wheel cylinders often have dust boots over the ends to keep contamination out.

Slide 2 of 3

Inspection: Pull the Boots Back

You can't tell if a wheel cylinder is leaking just by looking at the outside. To inspect properly:

  • Remove the drum and inspect the wheel cylinder closely.
  • Pull each dust boot back gently.
  • Look for brake fluid behind the boot. Any fluid means the cup seals are leaking.
  • Check for rust or scoring on the cylinder bore (visible if cups are removed).
A common mistake: assuming a dry exterior means a dry wheel cylinder. The fluid often pools behind the dust boot before it ever drips out.
Slide 3 of 3

Replace, Don't Rebuild

Wheel cylinders can be rebuilt with new cup seals, but modern practice is almost always to replace the whole assembly.

  • Rebuild kits are increasingly hard to find.
  • New wheel cylinders are inexpensive.
  • A rebuilt cylinder with a pitted bore will leak again quickly.
  • Time spent rebuilding usually exceeds the cost difference.
Always replace wheel cylinders in pairs (both sides of the same axle), even if only one is leaking. The other side likely isn't far behind.
