The Whole Point of ABS

The ABS system exists for one reason: prevent wheels from locking up under hard braking.

Why locked wheels are bad: a locked wheel is sliding. A sliding wheel has less traction than a rolling wheel. And a locked wheel can't be steered.

The ABS module monitors wheel speed continuously. If a wheel is decelerating much faster than vehicle speed (about to lock), the module intervenes.

Slide 2 of 3

Three Pressure Phases

During an ABS event, the HCU (Hydraulic Control Unit) cycles each wheel through three distinct pressure phases:

  • Pressure Decrease (Dump): An isolation valve closes, blocking the driver's pedal pressure from reaching the wheel. A dump valve opens, releasing pressure from the caliper. The wheel speed recovers as it stops trying to lock.
  • Pressure Hold: Both valves are closed. Pressure stays at whatever level it was when the wheel was at threshold. No more pressure can reach the wheel, but none is released.
  • Pressure Increase: Isolation valve opens, dump valve stays closed. Pedal pressure returns to the wheel. The pump may also actively re-pressurize the line.
Slide 3 of 3

What the Driver Feels

During an ABS event, the driver experiences:

  • Pedal pulsing or vibration: the rapid valve cycling sends pressure pulses back up the brake lines to the pedal.
  • A buzzing or grinding sound: the ABS pump motor and valves making rapid hydraulic adjustments.
This is NORMAL behavior during ABS activation. Drivers sometimes release the pedal thinking something's wrong. Educate them: keep firm pressure on the pedal and let the system work.
