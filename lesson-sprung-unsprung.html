Why lighter wheels equal better handling

The Two Categories

Every vehicle's weight breaks into two categories based on where the springs are.

Sprung weight is everything supported by the springs - the frame, body, engine, transmission, fuel tank, passengers, and cargo. It rides on top of the suspension.

Unsprung weight is everything NOT supported by the springs - the wheels, tires, brakes, hubs, wheel bearings, and the lower parts of the suspension that move with the wheel.

Quick test

Ask: "When the wheel hits a bump, does this part move with the wheel?" If yes - unsprung. If no - sprung.

Slide 2 of 3
Why It Matters

When a wheel hits a bump, only the unsprung mass has to accelerate up and down. The lighter that unsprung mass, the faster the wheel can react to road surface changes.

Lower unsprung weight means:

  • Better grip - the tire stays in contact with the road longer
  • Better ride - less shock transmitted to the sprung mass
  • Better handling - the suspension reacts faster to inputs

This is why performance vehicles use aluminum control arms, forged wheels, and inboard brakes - all efforts to reduce unsprung mass.

Slide 3 of 3
The Suspension's Job

The whole point of a suspension system is to separate sprung weight from unsprung weight, while keeping the tire planted on the road.

Springs hold the sprung mass up. Shocks dampen spring oscillation. Control arms locate the wheel. Bushings allow controlled motion. Each component is doing its part to let the wheel move independently of the body.

Diagnostic tip

When a tire wears in unusual patterns (cupping, scalloping), it often means the wheel was bouncing off the road - shock or strut failure increases the unsprung mass's ability to skip the road surface.

Knowledge Check - Question 1 of 3
Which component is part of UNSPRUNG weight?
  • Engine
  • Body panels
  • Wheels and tires
  • Fuel tank
Correct. Wheels and tires sit below the springs and move with the road surface, so they are unsprung mass.
Not quite. Wheels and tires are unsprung - they are not supported by the springs. The engine, body, and fuel tank all sit on the springs.
Knowledge Check - Question 2 of 3
Why is lower unsprung weight desirable?
  • It makes the vehicle cheaper to manufacture
  • It lets the wheel react faster to road imperfections for better grip
  • It reduces the size of the engine needed
  • It increases the load capacity
Correct. Lower unsprung mass accelerates up and down faster, so the tire stays in contact with the road longer - which improves grip, handling, and ride quality.
Not quite. The reason is mechanical: less mass to accelerate vertically means the wheel can follow road surface changes faster, improving tire-to-road contact.
Knowledge Check - Question 3 of 3
A control arm is partially sprung and partially unsprung. Why?
  • It is made of two different materials
  • It pivots - the end at the chassis is sprung, the end at the wheel is unsprung
  • It only counts as unsprung when the vehicle is moving
  • It is always considered fully sprung
Correct. Control arms pivot. The inner mounting points are bolted to the chassis (sprung), while the outer end at the ball joint moves with the wheel (unsprung). Engineers typically count half its weight as each.
Not quite. It pivots. One end is fixed to the chassis (sprung) and the other end moves with the wheel (unsprung). Material and motion don't change this - geometry does.
