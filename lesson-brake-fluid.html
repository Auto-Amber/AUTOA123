Brake Fluid Properties and DOT Ratings

Brake fluid transmits hydraulic pressure through the brake lines. It must withstand extreme heat while remaining liquid and protecting rubber seals and metal components.

Brake fluid is rated by the DOT (Department of Transportation) based on its boiling points:

TypeDry Boil PointWet Boil PointNotes
DOT 3401°F / 205°C284°F / 140°CGlycol-based. Most common. Absorbs moisture.
DOT 4446°F / 230°C311°F / 155°CGlycol-based. Higher boiling point. ABS/ESP vehicles.
DOT 5500°F / 260°C356°F / 180°CSilicone-based. NOT compatible with DOT 3/4. Military/specialty use.
DOT 5.1500°F / 260°C356°F / 180°CGlycol-based. High performance. Compatible with DOT 3/4.
Never mix DOT 5 (silicone) with DOT 3, 4, or 5.1 (glycol-based). The fluids are incompatible and will cause seal damage and system failure.

Hygroscopic Nature and Moisture Contamination

DOT 3, 4, and 5.1 fluids are hygroscopic — they absorb moisture from the atmosphere over time. This is by design: it keeps water dispersed in the fluid rather than pooling in one spot where it could cause corrosion or a localized boiling point issue.

However, absorbed moisture lowers the boiling point significantly:

  • Fresh DOT 3: boils at 401°F (dry boiling point)
  • DOT 3 with 3.7% water: boils at only 284°F (wet boiling point)
  • A fluid boiling in a caliper creates a vapor bubble — compressible — causing sudden pedal fade
This is why brake fluid should be tested and replaced on a maintenance schedule — typically every 2 years or 24,000 miles, or whenever a moisture content tester reads above 3%.

DOT 5 (silicone) is not hygroscopic — it repels moisture. But water that enters the system pools at low points, causing localized corrosion and ice formation at low temperatures.

Brake Fluid Service and Contamination

Testing brake fluid:

  • Use a brake fluid test strip or refractometer to check moisture content
  • Electronic testers check boiling point directly — most accurate method
  • Never rely on visual inspection alone — contaminated fluid can look clear

Contaminated fluid symptoms:

  • Spongy pedal that worsens after hard stops (vapor lock)
  • Dark or discolored fluid in reservoir (moisture and oxidation)
  • Corroded brake lines or master cylinder bore
Petroleum contamination (brake fluid mixed with power steering fluid, motor oil, or any petroleum product) immediately swells rubber seals throughout the entire system. All rubber components — every caliper, wheel cylinder, hose, and master cylinder seal — must be replaced. This is not a flush — it is a complete system rebuild.

Flushing procedure: Always flush from the caliper furthest from the master cylinder first. Use the vehicle manufacturer's recommended fluid type — never upgrade to DOT 5 in a system designed for DOT 3/4.
