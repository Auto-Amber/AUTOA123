Why ABS Exists — The Physics of Braking

When a wheel locks up (stops rotating while the vehicle is still moving), the tire transitions from rolling friction to sliding friction.

  • Rolling friction coefficient: approximately 0.7–0.8 (high grip)
  • Sliding friction coefficient: approximately 0.4–0.6 (significantly lower)

A locked wheel actually produces less stopping force than a rolling wheel. Additionally:

  • A locked wheel cannot generate lateral (steering) force
  • The driver loses the ability to steer around the obstacle
  • A skidding tire wears rapidly in a flat spot
ABS goal: Keep each wheel rotating just at the edge of lockup — maintaining maximum friction and steering control simultaneously. ABS does not necessarily make a vehicle stop shorter on dry pavement — its primary benefit is maintaining steerability during emergency braking.

ABS Components

Every ABS system uses four key components:

Wheel Speed Sensors (WSS)
One per wheel. Monitor rotational speed of each wheel. Passive (magnetic) or active (Hall effect). Ring gear on hub or axle shaft.
ABS Control Module (ABSCM)
Monitors all four WSS signals 100+ times per second. Detects impending wheel lockup by comparing wheel speeds. Commands the modulator.
Hydraulic Control Unit (HCU)
Contains inlet and outlet solenoid valves plus a return pump. Controls hydraulic pressure to each wheel individually. 3 or 4 channel designs.
ABS Warning Light
Illuminates when a fault is detected. During normal operation, lights briefly at startup (bulb check) then goes out. ABS still works normally even during wheel-speed sensor learning.

The ABS cycle: Hold → Reduce → Increase pressure — repeated 8–12 times per second at each wheel independently.

ABS Operation and Diagnosis

The ABS pressure cycle:

  1. Hold: Inlet valve closes — no more pressure increase at that wheel
  2. Reduce: Outlet valve opens — pressure drops, wheel begins to rotate again
  3. Increase: Inlet valve reopens — pressure builds again toward lockup threshold
  4. Cycle repeats 8–12 times per second until wheel is stable or vehicle stops

The pedal pulsation and noise a driver feels during ABS activation is normal and expected. Drivers should be instructed to: hold firm pressure on the pedal and steer around the hazard — do not pump the brakes.

ABS diagnosis sequence: Scan for DTCs → identify the circuit → check wheel speed sensor, ring gear, wiring, and connector before replacing the module or HCU. WSS failures are the most common ABS fault.

A faulty wheel speed sensor signal causes the module to detect a false "lockup" — triggering ABS at low speeds or preventing ABS from activating when needed.
