The Four Code Categories

OBD-II diagnostic trouble codes start with a letter that tells you what system the code is for:

  • P-codes: Powertrain (engine, transmission, emissions)
  • B-codes: Body (airbags, lighting, instrument cluster)
  • C-codes: Chassis (ABS, traction control, suspension)
  • U-codes: Network communication (modules can't talk to each other)
U-codes don't tell you a sensor is bad. They tell you a module cannot communicate with another module. The actual fault could be anywhere on the network: wiring, connector, power, ground, or another module.

CAN Bus Basics

Most modern vehicles use Controller Area Network (CAN) to let modules share data. Key points:

  • CAN uses two wires: CAN High and CAN Low.
  • Signals are differential. Both wires carry the same data flipped in voltage.
  • Many modules share the bus: ECM, TCM, ABS, BCM, etc.
  • One bad module that's pulling the bus low can take the entire network down.
U-codes in the ABS module like 'lost communication with ECM' don't mean the ECM is bad. They mean the ABS module isn't hearing from the ECM. The problem could be the ECM, the wiring, or even another module flooding the bus.
Slide 3 of 3

Diagnosing U-Codes

When you pull a U-code, here's the basic approach:

  • Scan all modules: Find out which modules are reporting communication faults. If only one module is offline, that module (or its wiring) is suspect. If many modules are reporting "lost comm with module X," that module X is likely the issue.
  • Check for power and ground at the offline module: No power means the module can't talk at all.
  • Inspect CAN wiring: Look for damage at flex points and connectors. Check terminating resistors are intact.
  • Scope the bus: A two-channel scope can show whether the CAN signals are healthy or being pulled low by a bad module.

Never just clear U-codes and hope they don't return. They almost always do, and they usually indicate a real network problem that will affect multiple systems.
