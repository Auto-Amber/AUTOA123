The Metering Device's Job

The metering device sits between the high side and low side. Its job is to drop refrigerant pressure quickly so the refrigerant can absorb heat at the evaporator. It also controls how much refrigerant enters the evaporator.

Why flow control matters: Too much refrigerant floods the evaporator -- liquid can reach the compressor and cause damage (slugging). Too little starves the evaporator and reduces cooling. The metering device keeps flow matched to demand.

There are two types in common use. The thermostatic expansion valve (TXV) is variable -- it constantly adjusts flow based on load. The orifice tube is fixed -- it is just a calibrated hole that never changes size. Each system type requires a different approach to diagnosis.

TXV -- Thermostatic Expansion Valve

The TXV is a self-regulating valve that senses the temperature and pressure of the refrigerant leaving the evaporator (called superheat) and adjusts its opening to keep superheat constant -- typically 8-12 degrees F above the boiling point.

TXV system identification clues:
-- Receiver-drier on the high side (between condenser and TXV)
-- No accumulator (or a small one integrated into the evaporator)
-- TXV located at the evaporator inlet, sometimes inside the dash
-- Systems that use a TXV cycle the compressor clutch on and off based on a pressure switch or thermostat

TXV failure modes: stuck closed (no cooling, very low suction pressure, high discharge pressure relative to ambient), stuck open (flooding, liquid reaching compressor, frost on suction line before compressor), or erratic hunting (alternating between high and low suction pressure).

Testing a TXV: Cover the evaporator outlet sensor bulb with ice to close the valve -- suction pressure should drop. Warm it with your hand to open the valve -- suction pressure should rise. If there is no response, the TXV is stuck.

Orifice Tube System

The orifice tube is a fixed restriction -- a small plastic tube with a calibrated screen and brass orifice. Because it cannot regulate flow, the system uses a different strategy: the compressor clutch cycles on and off (controlled by a pressure cycling switch) to prevent evaporator freeze-up.

Orifice tube system identification clues:
-- Accumulator on the low side (after the evaporator, before the compressor)
-- No receiver-drier
-- Orifice tube located in the liquid line, usually near the evaporator inlet
-- Compressor clutch cycles frequently at idle in hot weather

The accumulator holds excess liquid refrigerant so it cannot slug the compressor. The desiccant inside also absorbs moisture.
