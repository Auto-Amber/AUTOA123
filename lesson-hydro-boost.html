How Hydro-Boost Works

Hydro-boost uses pressurized power steering fluid instead of engine vacuum to assist the brake pedal. It was developed by Bendix and is common on diesel trucks, turbocharged vehicles, and any platform where engine vacuum is unavailable or insufficient.

The system uses the power steering pump as its pressure source:

  • Power steering pump produces 1,200–2,000 PSI
  • Fluid flows through the hydro-boost unit and on to the steering gear
  • A spool valve inside the booster controls flow
At rest: fluid flows through unrestricted — no pressure builds in the boost chamber.
Pedal pressed: spool valve closes, fluid pressure builds in the boost chamber, pushing the output piston into the master cylinder.

The boost force is proportional to pedal input — the harder you press, the more the spool valve restricts flow and the higher the pressure in the boost chamber.

The Accumulator and Reserve Braking

One of the most important components in the hydro-boost system is the accumulator. It stores pressurized fluid so that reserve braking is available even when the power steering pump is not running.

Two accumulator designs:

Spring-Loaded Accumulator
A spring compresses to store fluid. Provides 2–3 power-assisted stops after pump stops. Simpler design.
Gas-Charged Accumulator
A nitrogen charge pre-loads a diaphragm. Higher energy storage. More consistent reserve pressure.

To test the accumulator: turn engine off, then apply the brake pedal several times. You should get at least 2 full power-assisted applications. If the pedal immediately becomes hard: accumulator has failed or fluid is low.

Always depressurize the accumulator before disconnecting any hydro-boost hydraulic line. Pump the brake pedal 10+ times with the engine off to release stored pressure.

Diagnosing Hydro-Boost

Hydro-boost problems are almost always traced to the power steering system, not the booster unit itself. Diagnose in this order:

StepCheckSpec / Expected
1Power steering fluid levelFull — low fluid is the most common cause
2Drive belt condition and tensionCorrect tension — slipping belt reduces pump output
3Power steering pump pressure1,200–2,000 PSI (vehicle-specific — use ShopKey)
4Fluid volumeApproximately 2 gallons/min at full lock
5All hose connections and booster bodyNo leaks — even small leaks reduce assist

Common symptoms and causes:

  • Hard pedal: Low fluid, slipping belt, weak pump, or accumulator failure
  • Pedal pulsates with steering wheel turn: Normal — pump load affects boost momentarily
  • Groaning noise during braking: Low fluid or air in the power steering system
