Common Symbols and What They Mean

A wiring diagram is a map of a circuit drawn in a standard way so any technician can follow it. Before you can trace a circuit, you need to recognize the symbols. ShopKey uses ISO/SAE standard symbols for most domestic and import vehicles.

Symbol (text)ComponentWhat to know
---/---Switch (open)Gap = open = no current flow. Closed switch = line through both contacts.
~M~MotorCircle with M. Two wires: supply and ground. Some show a third for feedback.
|||||Ground symbolLines narrowing to a point. Connects to chassis. Number after G identifies the ground splice (G101, G201, etc.)
--[R]--Resistor / BulbRectangle or zigzag. Bulb is a circle with a filament symbol inside.
--(K)--RelayCoil + contact symbol. 85/86 = coil. 30 = common. 87/87a = normally open/closed.
--[F]--FuseRectangle with a curved line inside. Amperage rating labeled next to it.
--.--ConnectorDot at the junction = wires are connected. Crossing lines without a dot = wires cross but do NOT connect.
Dot vs. no dot: This is the most common misread. Two wires crossing WITH a dot are connected at a splice. Two wires crossing WITHOUT a dot pass over each other with no connection.

How to Trace a Circuit

Every circuit in a wiring diagram flows from a power source (battery or fuse) through a load (motor, lamp, solenoid) and back to ground. To trace a circuit correctly, always start at the power source and follow the current path.

Wire code reading (ShopKey): Wire labels include color and circuit number. Example: 18 DK GRN/WHT 240 = 18-gauge wire, dark green with white tracer, circuit 240. The circuit number links to the system it belongs to in the diagram index.

When a diagram spans multiple pages, ShopKey uses connector references to show where a wire continues. A flag with a letter (C102, C205) refers to a connector shown on another page. The connector view shows pin positions and wire colors entering each side.

Diagnostic use: Before probing anything on the vehicle, use the diagram to identify the best test points -- where you expect to find voltage, where ground should be present, and what pin number on the connector to access. This prevents unnecessary disassembly.

Using ShopKey Diagrams for Diagnosis

ShopKey (Mitchell 1) organizes wiring diagrams by system. Start with the symptom, go to the relevant system diagram, and build a mental model of the circuit before you touch the vehicle.

ShopKey diagram workflow:
1. Select year/make/model
2. Go to Electrical > Wiring Diagrams > select the system (Battery, Starting, Charging, Lighting, etc.)
3. Identify the circuit components on the diagram
4. Locate the fuse, the ground reference (G-number), and the connector IDs
5. Use the Component Location views to find physical locations on the vehicle

The diagram tells you what should be present at each point. Your DVOM tells you what is present. The gap between expected and measured is where the fault lives.

Two things diagrams cannot tell you:

  • Whether a connector is corroded or a terminal has backed out of the housing
  • Whether a wire has chafed against a chassis component and grounded internally

Those require physical inspection alongside the diagram work. The diagram narrows your search to the right connector and wire; your eyes and hands complete the diagnosis.

Task list connection: ASE task VI.A-10 -- Use wiring diagrams during diagnosis of electrical circuit problems. This task applies to nearly every lab in the course.
