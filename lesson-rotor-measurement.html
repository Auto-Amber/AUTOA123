Rotor Measurement: Thickness, Variation & Runout

Three Measurements, Three Tools

You don't measure a rotor with just one tool. Three different measurements tell three different stories:

  • Thickness: measured with a brake rotor micrometer (one anvil pointed, one flat). Tells you if the rotor has enough material left.
  • Thickness variation (parallelism): measure thickness at 8+ points around the rotor. Variation between points tells you if the rotor is parallel.
  • Lateral runout: measured with a dial indicator on the rotor face while rotating. Tells you if the rotor is straight or wobbles.
Slide 2 of 3

MIN vs MACHINE-TO

Stamped on every rotor are two important specs:

MIN (or Minimum Thickness, or Discard Thickness): the absolute minimum thickness the rotor can be after wear. Below this, replace the rotor. Don't even try to machine it.
MACHINE-TO: the minimum thickness the rotor can be when you machine it. This leaves enough material for normal wear before the rotor hits MIN.

If you machine a rotor down to MIN, it has no remaining life. Always machine to MACHINE-TO at minimum.

Slide 3 of 3

What Excessive Readings Mean

Common rotor measurement problems:

  • Thickness below MIN: replace the rotor. Don't machine.
  • Excessive thickness variation: causes pedal pulsation. May be machineable if rotor has material to spare.
  • Excessive lateral runout: causes intermittent pulsation and rapid pad wear. Check hub face for rust before condemning the rotor.
A common runout spec is around 0.002 inches at the rotor face. Always check the service manual.
