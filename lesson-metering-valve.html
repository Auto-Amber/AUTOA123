What the Metering Valve Does

The metering valve (also called the hold-off valve) is found on vehicles with front disc and rear drum brakes. It delays hydraulic pressure to the front disc brakes during the very beginning of a brake application.

Why is a delay needed?

  • Rear drum brakes have return springs that hold the shoes off the drum at rest
  • Before drum brakes can generate any braking force, the wheel cylinder must extend enough to overcome those return springs
  • Disc brake calipers respond immediately — no springs to overcome, pistons are always near the rotor
Without a metering valve: during light stops, the front discs would do all the work before the rear drums even engaged — causing brake bias way too far forward and uneven wear.

The metering valve holds front disc pressure near zero until rear drum pressure is high enough to overcome the return springs — then releases to allow full front pressure.

Metering Valve Operation

The metering valve contains a small spring-loaded poppet that blocks front brake pressure until a set pressure threshold is reached:

  1. 0–3 PSI: Valve open — allows low-pressure fluid to pass (prevents corrosion and keeps seals lubricated)
  2. 3–75 PSI (approx): Valve closed — blocks front disc pressure while rear drum pressure builds and overcomes return springs
  3. Above split point: Valve opens fully — front disc and rear drum brake together

The exact hold-off pressure varies by vehicle — typically 60–135 PSI. Always verify with ShopKey.

Metering valves are only needed on front disc / rear drum combinations. Vehicles with four-wheel disc brakes do NOT use a metering valve — both front and rear calipers respond at the same rate.

On many vehicles, the metering valve is integrated into the combination valve along with the proportioning valve and brake warning switch.

Metering Valve Failure and Diagnosis

Failure modes:

FailureSymptom
Stuck closed (will not open)Front brakes not applying — extreme rear brake bias, rear drums overworked, long stopping distance
Stuck open (holds off nothing)Front discs apply before rear drums engage — front brake dominance, rear shoes underused
Leaking past sealFluid leaking from combination valve — low pedal, warning light

Testing the metering valve:

  • Install a T-fitting and pressure gauge in the front brake line before and after the metering valve
  • Apply light brake pressure — upstream pressure should build while downstream reads zero (valve holding)
  • Increase pressure — both should equalize above the split point (valve opened)
Most metering valve failures show up as uneven brake bias — front pads wearing much faster or slower than rear shoes. Any time brake wear is significantly mismatched front-to-rear, check the metering valve function.
