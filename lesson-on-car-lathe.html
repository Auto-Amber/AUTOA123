On-Car Brake Lathe (Pro-Cut)
Progress · 0%
Slide 1 of 3

Why On-Car Is Better

A bench (off-car) brake lathe machines the rotor as a standalone part. An on-car lathe machines the rotor while it's still bolted to the hub.

On-car cutting matches the rotor to the actual hub it will spin on. Even if the rotor is perfectly machined on a bench, hub imperfections can introduce runout when bolted back up. On-car cutting eliminates that.

For modern vehicles with tight runout tolerances (under 0.002"), on-car lathing is the only reliable way to deliver a true rotor every time.

Slide 2 of 3

Runout Compensation

The Pro-Cut and similar on-car lathes have a built-in runout compensation feature. Before cutting, the lathe head measures the rotor as it spins and detects any wobble. It then adjusts the cutter to match.

  • The cutter moves WITH the wobble so it cuts a flat surface regardless of hub-side runout.
  • After cutting, the rotor is true to the hub it spins on (the only reference that matters).
  • This is why on-car lathed rotors typically come in well under 0.001" runout.
Slide 3 of 3

Setup Procedure

Quick overview of the Pro-Cut setup. Always follow the actual training procedure for your specific lathe.

  • Vehicle on lift, wheel and caliper off (caliper hung, not by the hose).
  • Bolt the lathe carriage to the caliper bracket using the appropriate adapter.
  • Position the cutter heads on both sides of the rotor.
  • Run the runout compensation routine.
  • Set cut depth, engage the cutter, and let the lathe do its work.
  • Measure final thickness and verify it's at or above MACHINE-TO spec.
If the rotor is at or near MIN before cutting, replace it. Cutting a thin rotor leaves you with no wear margin and can cause rapid pulsation return.
