Why SRS Work Is High-Stakes

The supplemental restraint system exists to save lives. An airbag deploys in milliseconds at forces sufficient to cause serious injury if a technician or bystander is in the deployment path. Every SRS service procedure starts with one rule: disable the system before touching any component.

CRITICAL SAFETY RULE: Never probe SRS wiring with a standard DVOM or test light. The small current from a meter or light can trigger deployment. Use only SRS-rated diagnostic equipment. Never connect or disconnect SRS harness connectors with the ignition on.

The SRS system includes:

  • Airbag modules -- the bag and inflator assembly; contain a pyrotechnic squib that ignites rapidly to fill the bag with nitrogen gas
  • SRS control module (ACM/SDM) -- monitors crash sensors and fires the appropriate modules when threshold is met; also stores DTCs
  • Crash sensors -- measure deceleration; may be in the module or distributed (satellite sensors in door pillars, bumper)
  • Clock spring (spiral cable) -- maintains electrical connection to steering wheel airbag and horn through full steering rotation
  • Seat belt pretensioners -- pyrotechnic devices that retract the belt instantly at impact

Disabling the SRS -- Required Procedure

The standard disable procedure eliminates stored electrical energy in the SRS backup capacitor, which can hold enough charge to deploy a module even with the battery disconnected.

Standard SRS disable procedure:
1. Turn the ignition off.
2. Disconnect the negative battery cable (some manufacturers also specify positive).
3. Wait a minimum of 3 minutes (some manufacturers specify up to 10 minutes) for the backup capacitor to discharge.
4. Do not reconnect the battery or turn the ignition on during service.

Always look up the vehicle-specific procedure in ShopKey -- some vehicles have additional steps (e.g., disconnecting a specific SRS fuse or connector before battery disconnect).

When is SRS disable required?

  • Steering wheel, column, or clock spring replacement
  • Dashboard or instrument panel removal
  • Any work near airbag module locations (A-pillar, dash, door, roof, seats)
  • Seat belt pretensioner inspection or replacement
  • Any welding or cutting near the vehicle (heat can trigger deployment)

Clock Spring, DTCs, and Post-Repair Verification

The clock spring (also called a spiral cable or cable reel) is a coiled flat ribbon cable inside the steering column that allows the steering wheel to rotate while maintaining continuous electrical connections to the airbag, horn, and steering wheel controls.

Clock spring failure symptoms:
-- Horn does not work (most common first symptom)
-- Steering wheel audio, cruise, or phone controls intermittent or dead
-- SRS warning light on with codes for driver airbag circuit
-- Clicking or grinding noise from column during steering

Clock spring replacement rules:

  • Center the steering before removal -- mark the wheel position, remove it, and center the clock spring to its mid-point (usually indicated by a tab or marking). Install with wheels straight ahead.
  • Never force rotation past the stops -- you will break the ribbon cable inside the new unit.
Post-repair: After any SRS work, retrieve and clear all DTCs with a scan tool rated for SRS systems. Verify the SRS warning light extinguishes after reconnecting the battery. A light that stays on means a fault is still present -- do not return the vehicle until the system is verified.
