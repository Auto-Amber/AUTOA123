ABS Bleed Procedure: Scan Tool Required

Why Conventional Bleeding Isn't Enough

The ABS Hydraulic Control Unit (HCU) contains isolation valves, dump valves, accumulators, and pump chambers that hold small pockets of brake fluid.

Air trapped in those internal cavities is unreachable by bleeding at the wheel cylinders. You can bleed all four wheels until the fluid runs perfectly clear and still have a spongy pedal because air is hiding inside the HCU.

This is when you need a scan tool with ABS bleed capability.

Slide 2 of 3

What the Scan Tool Does

The scan tool sends bidirectional commands to the ABS module. The module then cycles its internal valves and runs the pump:

  • Solenoid valves open and close rapidly.
  • The pump motor activates and circulates fluid through the internal passages.
  • Air pockets in the HCU are flushed through and out to the wheel cylinders.
  • You then bleed each wheel as usual to expel the air.
Without a scan tool, the valves stay in their default rest position. The fluid you push through goes around the trapped air pockets, not through them.
Slide 3 of 3

When You Need It

Use the scan tool ABS bleed procedure when:

  • You replaced the ABS module or HCU.
  • You replaced the master cylinder.
  • You opened any brake line between the master cylinder and the HCU.
  • The pedal stays spongy after conventional bleeding at all four wheels.
  • The vehicle's service manual specifies it for the repair you performed.

For a routine brake fluid flush (no air introduced) on most vehicles, conventional bleeding is sufficient. The scan tool procedure is for when air got past the master cylinder.
