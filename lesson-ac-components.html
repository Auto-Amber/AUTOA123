Compressor -- The Heart of the System

The compressor does two things: it pumps refrigerant around the system and it raises refrigerant pressure so it can condense at outdoor temperatures. Without high pressure on the discharge side, the condenser could not release heat into ambient air.

Common compressor types:

  • Swash plate (piston): most common on older vehicles; variable displacement versions adjust stroke based on load.
  • Scroll: common on newer vehicles; two spiral elements mesh together to compress vapor; quieter and more efficient.
  • Electric compressor (EV/hybrid): runs independently of the engine; requires HV-rated oil and HV safety precautions.
Compressor clutch: On belt-driven compressors, an electromagnetic clutch engages the drive plate to the compressor body when the A/C is requested. A failed clutch (no gap, incorrect gap, coil open) is a common cause of no-cool complaints. Gap spec is typically 0.015-0.040 in -- check the service manual.
Oil charge: Every time a component is replaced, the correct amount of new compressor oil must be added. Too little oil destroys the compressor. Too much oil reduces heat transfer and can cause slugging. Always look up the spec by component.

Condenser and Evaporator

Both the condenser and evaporator are heat exchangers -- finned-tube assemblies that allow heat to transfer between the refrigerant inside and the air flowing across the fins. The direction of heat flow is opposite at each one.

CondenserEvaporator
LocationIn front of the radiator (outside the cabin)Inside the HVAC box behind the dash
Refrigerant state inHot high-pressure vaporCold low-pressure liquid/mixture
Refrigerant state outHigh-pressure liquidLow-pressure vapor
Heat directionRefrigerant releases heat to outside airRefrigerant absorbs heat from cabin air
Air across fins feelsHot (you can feel the heat in front of the car)Cold (this is what the vents blow)

Condenser service notes: Bugs, leaves, and road debris reduce airflow and cause high-side pressure to spike. Always inspect and clean the condenser before condemning other components. Bent fins can be straightened with a fin comb.

Evaporator service notes: The evaporator naturally collects moisture from the cabin air -- the drain tube must be clear or water will drip on the floor mat. Evaporator replacement requires significant disassembly and is one of the most labor-intensive A/C jobs.

Pressure Switches and A/C Controls

A/C systems use pressure switches to protect components and control compressor operation. Knowing which switch does what is essential for electrical diagnosis.

Switch/SensorLocationWhat it does
Low pressure switchLow sideOpens compressor clutch circuit if pressure drops too low (refrigerant loss, restriction). Prevents compressor damage from running dry.
High pressure switchHigh sideOpens compressor clutch circuit if pressure exceeds safe limit (condenser blockage, overcharge). Prevents system rupture.
Pressure cycling switch (OT systems)Low sideCycles compressor clutch on/off to control evaporator temperature and prevent freeze-up.
Ambient temp sensorFront of vehicleInhibits A/C operation when outside temp is too cold (below approx. 35 degrees F) -- refrigerant pressure too low for safe operation.
Diagnosis shortcut: No compressor clutch engagement with A/C requested? Check power at the clutch coil first (jumper test), then work backward through the pressure switches and relay. A low-side pressure switch open from low refrigerant is one of the most common "no A/C" causes.
