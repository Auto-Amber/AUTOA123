How the Recirculating Ball Gearbox Works

The recirculating ball steering gear was the dominant steering design on American vehicles through the 1990s and remains common on full-size trucks and SUVs. Unlike rack and pinion, which moves the rack directly, the gearbox converts steering wheel rotation into movement at the pitman arm, which then connects to the steering linkage.

Inside the box: The steering shaft has a worm gear. Steel ball bearings ride in the groove between the worm and a ball nut, recirculating through a return tube. The ball nut has gear teeth on one side that mesh with a sector gear (also called the pitman shaft). As the worm turns, the ball nut moves, which rotates the sector gear, which moves the pitman arm.

The ball bearings eliminate sliding friction -- the recirculating balls roll rather than slide, which is why the system requires less effort than an older worm-and-sector design and lasts longer.

Steering Linkage Connected to the Gearbox

The gearbox output is just one part of the system. The pitman arm connects to a relay rod (also called a center link or drag link depending on design), which connects to the idler arm on the passenger side. Tie rods run from the relay rod to each steering knuckle.

Diagnosis approach: Have a helper slowly turn the wheel back and forth while you watch and feel each joint. Loose joints show movement before the wheel movement reaches the tire. Work from the gearbox outward -- gearbox lash first, then pitman, idler, relay rod, tie rods.

Gearbox Adjustment and Replacement

Unlike rack and pinion, the recirculating ball gearbox has two adjustments that compensate for wear: worm bearing preload (controls drag on the steering shaft rotation) and sector lash (controls play between the sector gear and ball nut in the center/straight-ahead position).

On-vehicle adjustment procedure (general):
1. Disconnect the pitman arm from the relay rod (so you are only feeling gearbox resistance, not the whole linkage).
2. Adjust worm bearing preload first -- rotate the steering shaft and feel for smooth, consistent drag with no roughness.
3. Adjust sector lash -- set the box to straight ahead, then adjust the top cover bolt until just-perceptible resistance is felt through center, with no binding through the full range of travel.
4. Never adjust the sector lash tighter than the spec to mask worn components -- replace the gearbox if adjustment cannot bring it into spec.

Power steering on recirculating ball vehicles uses a hydraulic assist valve integrated into or adjacent to the gearbox. The same pump, hoses, and pressure diagnosis concepts apply as with rack and pinion power steering.
